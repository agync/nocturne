A memory that lives on your computer

It sleeps on it. You wake to one page.

During the day you keep things: a photo of a letter, a voice note after a call, a pasted message. Overnight Nocturne reads them on this computer and writes you a single page of what needs you, what changed and what it let go.

How a day goes

Keep now. Think later. Read once.

  1. Any time

    Keep

    Press Ctrl Shift Space, then type, talk, paste or drop a file. No folders, no tags. Nocturne says “Kept.” and you go back to what you were doing.

  2. From about 01:00

    Nocturne tidies

    While the computer is plugged in and idle, it reads what you kept, links it to what it already knows, notices what changed and lets go of what you no longer use. A laptop that slept catches up when it wakes.

  3. 07:00, or when you choose

    One page

    Read it, listen to it, or print it on A4 or an 80 mm receipt. In between, ask anything and Nocturne answers from what you kept.

In the app

What living with it looks like.

These are Nocturne’s own screens, running on demo data: a household with a school trip, a moved dentist appointment and a grandmother’s new phone number.

Keep

One box for everything.

Type a line, paste a message, drop a photo or a PDF, or hold Space in the empty box to talk. Keeping works at once, even before Nocturne has read it. Recently kept shows what it made of each thing, and says plainly when it could not read one.

Ask

Answers with receipts.

Ask in plain words. Nocturne answers only from what you kept and shows the letter, note or recording each part came from. When two things you kept disagree it asks you which is right instead of guessing, and when it does not know, it says so.

The morning page

One page that shows its working.

At 07:00, or when you choose, the page says what needs you, what changed and what Nocturne let go. Each line points to the letter, note or message it came from. When two things you kept disagree, like an appointment card and a later voice note, the page shows both and asks which is right.

Let go

Forgetting you can see.

Things you have not used fade after 30 days and are let go after 90. Nothing is deleted quietly: bring anything back with one click, or forget it completely, which always asks first.

Away from home

Your page on your phone, if you want it.

Away from home is optional and stays off until you set it up. Once it is on, a phone you pair can reach this computer over the internet to ask, keep a note, a photo or a voice note, and read today’s page. You choose how: Simple, through Cloudflare, or Most private, through Tailscale. What you ask and keep is locked so neither company can read it, though each can see when your phone connects. A phone cannot delete, export or change settings, and things you marked private stay on this computer.

What it promises

Your life stays on your computer.

Nocturne goes online only to download what you agree to: its mind at setup and any update you allow. Privacy in the app lists each download with the day and the size.

Nothing to sign in to
No account, no analytics, no crash reports, no cloud copy.
Every line has a receipt
Each line on the page and each answer points back to the photo, note or message it came from.
It asks when unsure
Two things you kept disagree? You get a choice, not a guess.
It forgets on purpose
Old things fade and are let go, out loud. Bring any back, or forget something completely.
One quiet note a day
When the page is ready, one note says so and how many things need you. Never what you kept.
Checked, not just claimed
Before a release, Nocturne is run with its network cut off from everything but this computer, and any attempt to reach further fails the check.
Your phone, only if you ask
Away from home is the one exception. It sends to your own phones and is off until you set it up.

The engine

Built on

Nocturne’s thinking runs on Embeddable Lemonade, the portable build of Lemonade, the open-source local AI server. Lemonade runs the models that read your photos, hear your voice notes, write your page and answer your questions, here on this computer.

At setup Nocturne asks Lemonade what the computer has to work with and installs the setup that fits. Lemonade is free under the Apache 2.0 licence and built by its community, with optimisations by AMD engineers. Nocturne would not exist without it.

Requirements

It fits the computer you have.

Linux on a 64-bit Intel or AMD computer, and Macs with Apple Silicon on macOS 26 or newer. At setup Nocturne checks the memory and free space and offers the largest setup the computer carries comfortably. Every setup does everything; smaller ones answer more simply and tidy more slowly.

Large

60 GB+ memory · 28 GB space

Clearer answers to harder questions.

Standard

28 GB+ memory · 11 GB space

Many recent laptops with 32 GB.

Small

14 GB+ memory · 7 GB space

Same page. The overnight tidy takes longer.

Tiny

7 GB+ memory · 5.2 GB space

Made for 8 GB computers. Simpler answers that may miss details.

The one-off download at setup is between about 3 GB and 26 GB, depending on the setup. After that, Nocturne works with no internet at all.
