Help with Nocturne.

Nocturne keeps everything on your own computer or phone, so no help desk can see your memory. Help starts with Nocturne itself and its guide, and the people who make it are a message away.

Start here.

Ask Nocturne

On a computer, type your question in the ask box, like how do I add a phone. Nocturne answers from the guide that comes with it and shows the part it used.

On your phone

Open This phone, then Help: what this phone can do, its permissions with a way to change them, and the privacy policy.

Read the guide

Installing, your computer’s desktop, Away from home, Online answers and your data.

Common questions.

My page didn’t come
Nocturne writes it in the hours before your page time, while the computer is on, plugged in and not in use. If it was off or asleep, Nocturne catches up when it wakes. The guide explains each desktop.
I lost my phone
If it was paired with your computer, your memory is on the computer: open Away from home there, choose See phones, then Remove beside it. If the phone kept your memory on its own, install Nocturne on the new phone and choose I have a backup.
I forgot my passphrase
No one can open a locked backup without its passphrase or recovery key, not even the people who make Nocturne. Make a new backup with a passphrase you can keep.
How do I delete everything?
Choose Delete everything… in Privacy on a computer, or in This phone on a phone that keeps your memory itself. Nocturne’s makers hold nothing of yours, so there is nothing else to ask for. A service you chose for answers keeps what it received under its own rules.
What does Nocturne collect?
Nothing. No account, no analytics, no crash reports. The privacy policy says exactly what can leave your computer or phone, and only when you turn it on.

Write to us.

Something not working, or a word that reads wrong? Email support@<domain>. A person reads every message. Say which computer or phone you use and what you saw; never send what you kept, your passphrase or your online service key.

Or open an issue on GitHub. Issues are public, so leave out anything personal.

To report a security problem, use GitHub’s private reporting, not a public issue.
