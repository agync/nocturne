The paired app now follows [Nocturne on Android and iPhone](/design/phone-apps.md), decision 1:
Today, Journal and Ask in a bar at the bottom, Keep as a floating button on Today and Journal, and
This phone at the right of the top bar. The phone page in a browser keeps its tiles (decision 4)
and gains one line on naming the phone: "Got the Nocturne app? Open it and choose Scan the code
instead."

Today is the phone edition of the page with last night's sleep and "In your calendar today" when a
phone brings them; the phone page in a browser shows neither (decision 4). The journal shows
appointments, workouts and the night on the day's timeline, "after {asleep} asleep" on the first
check-in, and a "From Health" card. The check-in says "Last night: {asleep} asleep" once the night
has come in.

After pairing, "Bring more from this phone?" offers the calendar and Health, each through its own
ask, and hides what the computer does not take. This phone lists the connection, what the phone
brings and when it last sent, its name and version, help, and Disconnect. Today carries the cards
for an update on either side; the version window drives them later (decision 12).

Nocturne appears in other apps' share menus for text, links, photos, PDFs and voice memos,
which open "Keep in Nocturne" over the app. A shared page or link is kept as untrusted web content,
as pasted web content is; what is shared waits on the phone while the computer is away. Receipts
for appointments name their calendar: "Calendar · Family · Sam’s phone". A long press on the icon
offers "Keep a note", "Record a voice note" and "Check in".

The allowed step in the app no longer shows the phone page's home screen steps, the phone's own
name is the default name, a new address can be scanned with the app's scanner, scrolled content no
longer shows under the status bar, sheets keep clear of the gesture bar, and the field being typed
in stays above the keyboard.
