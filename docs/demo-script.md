# Demo script

A 90-second video. The paper is the hero. The graph is the explanation.

## Shot list

| Time | Picture | Voice |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | A stack of thirty printed papers on a desk | "This is what my laptop remembered." |
| 0:08 | The laptop screen, the inbox | "Every AI forgets you. This one does not." |
| 0:15 | Drop a photo, a voice note, and a PDF into the inbox | "Three things. No cloud, no account." |
| 0:25 | Ask a question that needs all three | "What did I decide about the Berlin trip after that photo?" |
| 0:40 | The answer with three citations | "It answers, and it shows its work." |
| 0:50 | Cut to 2am, both devices busy | "At 2am it consolidated 412 events into 38 facts. It forgot 9." |
| 1:05 | The printer prints the paper | "And it printed this." |
| 1:10 | Read one line from the paper | "Needs you: the Berlin decision contradicts the June note." |
| 1:20 | Play 10 seconds of the audio edition | "It can read it to you." |
| 1:30 | A terminal: `ss -tupn` shows nothing outbound | "Zero cloud. Zero accounts." |
| 1:40 | End card | "Nocturne. Open source. One command." |

## What to prepare

- Thirty printed papers, one per day, from the real archive. Dates visible.
- One photo, one voice note, and one PDF that share a decision.
- A question whose answer needs all three. Write it down and rehearse it.
- A terminal window with `ss -tupn` or a packet capture. The point is that nothing leaves.
- A backup audio file, in case the printer or the speaker fails.

## What not to do

- Do not show the graph for more than three seconds. The paper is the product.
- Do not claim perfect recall. Show one forgotten fact and explain why it was forgotten.
- Do not use a synthetic dataset on camera. The demo is personal or it is not the point.
- Do not record the demo after the build is "finished". Record it at day 9, then improve it.

## The one line

If the viewer remembers one sentence, it should be:

> It remembers what matters, forgets what does not, and shows you both.
